    How sheet-music practice works

    Choose a clef and perform each note through your microphone. The report compares its start, end, and pitch with the score. Articulate each note cleanly and leave rests silent. You can also hold Space, the button, or the lane for each note; that scores rhythm only. Notes and count-in beats move toward the fixed play line from the right.

    Blue guides show the full note duration before it reaches the play line. Red overlays show the time you actually held the input, including early presses, late releases, and holds through rests.

    A whole note is hollow without a stem. A half note is hollow with a stem. A quarter note is filled with a stem; an eighth adds one flag, and a sixteenth adds two. When short notes are grouped, one beam replaces each eighth-note flag and two beams replace each sixteenth-note flag. Each value lasts half as long as the one before it.

    A curved tie joins consecutive notes of the same pitch across a barline. Attack the first note once and hold it continuously through the tied note.

    A dot adds half of the original duration. In 4/4, a quarter note lasts 1 beat, so a dotted quarter lasts 1 + ½ = 1½ beats. A half note lasts 2 beats, so a dotted half lasts 2 + 1 = 3 beats. Rests can have dots too.

    Negative milliseconds mean early; positive mean late. Attack and release errors are scored separately. Device and audio latency can affect measurements.

    What do these mean?

    An interval is the distance between two notes. One semitone is one piano-key step, including black keys. The distance stays the same when the notes start somewhere else or move downward. Some pitches have two names, such as F♯ and G♭. The interval determines which name is normally written, even though the sound is the same; a tritone can use either name.

    Interval Steps Example
    Unison 0 C → C
    Minor 2nd 1 C → D♭
    Major 2nd 2 C → D
    Minor 3rd 3 C → E♭
    Major 3rd 4 C → E
    Perfect 4th 5 C → F
    Tritone 6 C → F♯ / G♭
    Perfect 5th 7 C → G
    Minor 6th 8 C → A♭
    Major 6th 9 C → A
    Minor 7th 10 C → B♭
    Major 7th 11 C → B

    About

    440 Lab is a free, ad-free collection of simple tools for musical tuning, rhythm, pitch, and intonation ear training.

    Tuning and reference pitches

    Use the tuner to check the pitch of a note with your microphone, or play reference tones to practice matching and tuning by ear. The reference pitch is adjustable, so you are not limited to A440.

    Pitch and intonation ear training

    Practice hearing small differences in pitch and recognizing whether a note is sharp or flat, identifying a chromatic note without a reference, or identifying the distance between two notes. The exercises develop pitch discrimination, absolute and relative pitch recognition, and a more accurate sense of intonation.

    Metronome and rhythm practice

    Use the metronome for a steady beat while practicing, choose from common simple, compound, and odd meters, or tap a tempo to set the BPM by feel. Timing practice measures early and late taps. Sheet-music practice generates pitched phrases in four clefs and scores the timing and intonation of notes performed through a microphone.

    Simple by design

    440 Lab is designed to be a small, straightforward set of music practice tools: open it, choose a tool, and start practicing. Works well on desktop and mobile.

    Free forever

    440 Lab will always remain free, ad-free, and without in-app purchases. To run this site locally, download and unzip the latest release, which can be hosted on a webserver like nginx or by directly opening index.html.
